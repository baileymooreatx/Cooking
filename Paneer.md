# Homemade Paneer  

Making your own **paneer** is simple, requiring just **two main ingredients**: 
**full-fat milk** and an **acidic agent** (like lemon juice, vinegar, or citric
acid). Homemade paneer is often **softer and fresher** than store-bought
versions.

## Ingredients & Acid Selection

* **Milk**: Use **1 liter of whole (full-fat) milk**. Avoid skimmed, low-fat, or
  ultra-pasteurized (UHT) milk, as they yield less cheese and a grainy texture.
* **Acid**: You need about **2 to 4 tablespoons** of acid to curdle 1 liter of
  milk.
    * **White Vinegar**: The **most reliable** option. It creates **firm, smooth
      curds** quickly with **no aftertaste** after rinsing. Ideal for frying or
      grilling.
    * **Lemon Juice**: Common and natural, but can leave a **faint citrus 
      flavor** and requires a larger volume, potentially making the paneer 
      softer.
    * **Citric Acid**: The **professional choice** (1 tsp dissolved in water).
      It yields the **firmest texture** and highest quantity with zero flavor
      interference.
    * **Yogurt/Buttermilk**: Creates a **softer, creamier** paneer with a mild
      tang, best for desserts but less ideal for holding shape in curries.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Boil the Milk**: Pour the milk into a heavy-bottomed pot. Bring it to a 
   **rolling boil** over medium heat, stirring occasionally to prevent 
   scorching. Watch closely as it foams up.  
2. **Curdle**: Turn off the heat. Slowly add your chosen acid (e.g., vinegar or
   lemon juice) while stirring gently. You will see the milk separate almost
   immediately into **white curds** (solids) and **greenish-yellow whey** 
   (liquid). If the whey is still milky, add a little more acid until it runs
   clear.
3. **Strain and Rinse**: Line a colander with a **cheesecloth** (or clean
   muslin/cotton handkerchief) over a bowl to catch the whey (save it for dough
   or soups). Pour the curdled milk into the cloth. **Rinse the curds
   thoroughly with cold water** to remove any residual acid taste and stop the
   cooking process.
4. **Drain**: Gather the corners of the cloth and twist into a bundle. Squeeze
   gently to remove excess liquid. You can hang this bundle from a faucet for
   15–20 minutes to drain naturally.
5. **Press**: Place the bundle on a flat surface (like a plate or cutting
   board). Flatten it into a block shape with your hands. Place a heavy object (
   like a cast-iron skillet, a pot filled with water, or a heavy can) on top.
    * **Soft Paneer**: Press for **15 to 20 minutes**.
    * **Firm Paneer**: Press for **30 to 60 minutes**.
6. **Cube and Store**: Unwrap the block. It should be solid and hold its shape.
   Cut into cubes. Store in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 
   **3 to 4 days** (submerged in water to keep soft) or freeze for up to 3 
   months.
