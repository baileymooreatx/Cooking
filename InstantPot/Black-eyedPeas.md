# Black-eyed Peas

Cooking **soaked black-eyed peas** in an Instant Pot is significantly faster
than using dried beans, typically requiring just **4 to 5 minutes** of
high-pressure cooking time. Soaking the beans overnight or using the quick-soak
method ensures a creamy texture and reduces the likelihood of split skins. The
general liquid ratio for soaked peas is **2 cups of water or broth for every 1
cup of beans**.

## Ingredients

* 1 cup dried black-eyed peas, soaked overnight (or quick-soaked) and
  drained
* 2 to 3 cups liquid (water, chicken broth, or vegetable broth)
* 1 tablespoon oil (olive oil, vegetable oil, or bacon fat) to prevent
  foaming
* 1 teaspoon salt (adjust to taste; add after cooking if using salty broth)
* 1 onion (diced)
* 2 cloves garlic (minced)
* 1 bay leaf
* Ham hock or bacon

## Instructions

1. **Sauté Aromatics:** Set the Instant Pot to "Sauté." Add oil and
   cook onions, garlic, or other aromatics until softened. If using meat like
   bacon or ham hocks, brown them now. Press "Cancel" to turn off the heat.
2. **Add Beans and Liquid:** Add the drained, soaked black-eyed peas to the pot.
   Pour in 2 cups of liquid for a thicker consistency or 3 cups for a soupier
   result. Ensure the oil is included to prevent the pressure valve from
   clogging.
3. **Pressure Cook:** Secure the lid and set the steam release valve to
   "Sealing." Select "Manual" or "Pressure Cook" on **High Pressure** and set
   the timer for **4 to 5 minutes**. (4 minutes for firmer beans, 5 minutes for
   creamier texture).
4. **Natural Release:** Once the cooking cycle is complete, allow the pressure
   to release naturally for **10 minutes**. After 10 minutes, carefully flip the
   valve to "Venting" to release any remaining pressure.
5. **Season and Serve:** Open the lid, stir the peas, and add salt if you
   haven't already. If the mixture is too watery, use the "Sauté" function to
   simmer and thicken it to your liking.
