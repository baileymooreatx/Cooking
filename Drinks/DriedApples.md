# Dried Apples

A convection oven works well for drying apples because the fan circulates air,
speeding up and evening out moisture removal.

**Key settings:**

- **Temperature:** 135–145°F (57–63°C) is ideal. If your oven's minimum is
  higher (170–200°F), it still works but will take less time and may brown the
  edges.
- **Time:** Roughly **2–4 hours** at 170°F with convection, or **6–12 hours** if
  you can hold it at 135–145°F.
- **Setup:** Lay ¼-inch slices in a single, non-overlapping layer on a wire rack
  over a baking sheet for maximum airflow. Flip them halfway through.
- **Door:** If your oven can't go below ~150°F, prop the door open ~1 inch with
  a wooden spoon to let steam escape (check your manual first—some ovens shut
  off or disable the fan when the door opens).

**Done when:** Slices are leathery and pliable (for chewy) or crisp (for chips),
with no beads of moisture when pressed.

One caveat from extension sources: if your oven can't hold a temperature below ~
145°F, a dedicated dehydrator gives better results because the higher oven heat
can cause "case hardening" (the surface seals before the interior dries).
