# Celestial Seasonings Red Zinger

An exact, from‑scratch recreation isn't possible because 
[Celestial Seasonings' blend](https://celestialseasonings.com/products/red-zinger) 
is proprietary (it includes a "natural flavors" component and undisclosed
ratios), but we can [approximate](https://robotrecipes.co/best/Refreshing-Homemade-Red-Zinger-Tea-Recipe/) 
it with the listed botanicals. Here's a reasonable loose‑leaf approximation 
based on the official ingredient list and the flavor profile (tart hibiscus 
dominant, balanced by mint, citrus, and a hint of cherry/licorice).

## Approximate blend (makes ~1 cup of tea per 1–2 tsp)

| **Ingredient**                                  | **Amount per batch** (8‑cup pot)     | **Role**                               |
|:------------------------------------------------|:-------------------------------------|:---------------------------------------|
| Dried hibiscus (calyces, *Hibiscus sabdariffa*) | ¼–⅓ cup (the base, ~60–70% of blend) | Tartness, ruby‑red color               |
| Dried rosehips (cracked)                        | 2–3 tbsp                             | Fruity, vitamin C                      |
| Dried peppermint                                | 1–2 tbsp                             | Cool, refreshing note                  |
| Dried lemongrass                                | 1 tbsp                               | Lemony brightness                      |
| Dried orange peel (zest only, no pith)          | 1–2 tsp                              | Sweet citrus                           |
| Licorice root                                   | ½–1 tsp                              | Mild sweetness                         |
| Lemon verbena (or lemon myrtle)                 | ½–1 tsp                              | Citrus aroma                           |
| Wild cherry bark or dried red clover            | ½–1 tsp                              | Earthy cherry (optional, hard to find) |

Mix well and store in an airtight jar. Omit wild cherry bark if you can't source
it; the blend still works without it.

## Brewing One Cup

- Use **1–2 tsp of the blend per 8 oz of water**.
- Pour **freshly boiled water** over the blend (a full boil brings out the
  hibiscus color and tartness).
- **Steep 4–6 minutes** (hot) or longer/stronger for iced tea; over‑steeping
  past ~10 min turns it bitter.
- Strain, then sweeten with honey or sugar to taste.
- For iced tea: brew strong (e.g., 6–8 tsp in 4 cups water, steep 10 min), cool,
  dilute with cold water or add juice, and serve over ice.

## Brewing One Quart

A quart is 32 oz (4 cups), which is half the 8-cup batch from above. Here's the
scaled recipe:

| Ingredient                  | Amount    |
|-----------------------------|-----------|
| Dried hibiscus              | 2–3 tbsp  |
| Dried rosehips (cracked)    | 1–1½ tbsp |
| Dried peppermint            | ½–1 tbsp  |
| Dried lemongrass            | ½ tbsp    |
| Dried orange peel           | ½–1 tsp   |
| Licorice root               | ¼–½ tsp   |
| Lemon verbena               | ¼–½ tsp   |
| Wild cherry bark (optional) | ¼–½ tsp   |

## Brewing for a quart

- Combine all ingredients in a pot or teapot.
- Pour **1 quart of freshly boiled water** over the blend.
- **Steep 4–6 minutes**, then strain.
- Sweeten to taste.

For **iced tea**, brew the same blend in about 1½–2 cups of boiling water (steep
8–10 min), let cool, then top with cold water and ice to reach a full quart.
