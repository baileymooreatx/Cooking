# Types of Wheat Flour

The key difference in the different types of flour is the **protein content**, 
which determines how much gluten forms and thus the texture of the final baked 
good:

| Flour                 | Protein Content | Texture                                     | Best For                                       |
|-----------------------|-----------------|---------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| **Cake flour**        | 6–8%            | Very tender, fine crumb                     | Cakes, cupcakes, angel food cake               |
| **All-purpose flour** | 9–11%           | Balanced — neither too chewy nor too tender | Cookies, muffins, quick breads, general baking |
| **Bread flour**       | 11–14%          | Chewy, elastic, strong structure            | Yeast breads, pizza dough, bagels, focaccia    |

**Why it matters:** More protein → more gluten → stronger structure and chew.
Less protein → less gluten → softer, more tender crumb.

- **Cake flour** is made from soft wheat, finely milled, and often bleached. It
  produces light, delicate cakes.
- **All-purpose flour** is a blend of hard and soft wheat, making it the
  versatile middle ground.
- **Bread flour** is made from hard wheat, coarser in texture, and may contain
  added enzymes (like malted barley) to feed yeast. It builds the strong gluten
  network needed for high-rising, chewy breads.

**Substitution tips:** You can substitute all-purpose for bread flour 1:1 (
slightly less chew), or for cake flour (slightly denser). To mimic cake flour
from all-purpose, remove 2 tablespoons per cup and replace with cornstarch.
